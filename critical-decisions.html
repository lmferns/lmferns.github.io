Critical Decisions: Medical Equipment Service

A complete project for a fictional client, from needs analysis to evaluation. At its center is a branching Storyline scenario in which every safety decision plays out with a realistic consequence.

Corvalle Imaging Services and everyone in this project are fictional. The scenario, data and materials were created as a portfolio sample.

Analyze: needs analysis and action map

  • Needs analysis
  • Action mapping
  • Root cause

The problem

Technicians at a field service company passed their annual safety course, yet near-miss reports showed console shutdowns used in place of lockout, E-stop circuits jumpered between visits, and repeat calls after fuses were replaced without a root cause.

What I did

  • Set a measurable business goal: 30% fewer near-misses and repeat calls tied to energy control within 12 months.
  • Sorted five causes into knowledge, skill, environment and course design, and identified the two that training cannot fix.
  • Built an action map linking each on-the-job behavior to a practice decision and the minimum information needed to make it.
  • Recommended non-training fixes alongside the course: a site manager briefing and a standard lockout kit checklist.

Design: objectives, flow and storyboard

  • Merrill's First Principles
  • Gagné's Nine Events
  • Storyboarding

What I did

  • Wrote every objective at Apply or Analyze, so each one becomes a decision rather than a recall question.
  • Nested Merrill's First Principles inside Gagné's Nine Events, so each screen starts from a real task.
  • Designed three choices per decision: one best, one costly, and one unsafe that plays out its consequence before the learner retries.
  • Storyboarded every screen as a PowerPoint for direct Storyline import, with narration, triggers, variables, alt text and AI image prompts in the notes.

Build: Module 1 branching scenario

  • Variables
  • Text-to-speech
  • AI images
3 decisionseach with a best, a costly and an unsafe path

What I built

  • A branching scenario on a CT scanner that keeps shutting off, with three decisions on lockout, zero-energy verification and an open E-stop chain.
  • Personalization: learners enter their name at the start, and the scenario addresses them by it.
  • Safety, Trust and Time meters driven by variables, with an explanation of what each score means and why it changed.
  • Feedback layers that play out the consequence of an unsafe choice before the learner retries.
  • Clinical, directive narration generated with text-to-speech and closed captions, and scenes created with Storyline's AI image generator.

What I did

Designed a one-page job aid that matches the course exactly: the five lockout steps, the troubleshooting order for a repeated shutdown, a never list, escalation triggers and the return-to-service checklist. It is built to live in every service van and on every tablet.

05

Evaluate: results against targets

  • Kirkpatrick Levels 1 to 4
  • Learning data
  • Iteration
33% → 10%unsafe first choices after one data-driven revision

What I did

  • Set targets for every Kirkpatrick level in the needs analysis, before launch.
  • Tracked first-attempt choices at every decision, which showed exactly where technicians went wrong.
  • Revised one procedure screen based on the data, then measured the change with a second cohort.

Result (sample data)

Unsafe first choices at the zero-energy decision fell from 33% to 10% after a single content revision, and every Level 1 to 3 target set so far was met.
